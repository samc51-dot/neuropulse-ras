[ 01 / Research Abstract ]

Can Accented Rhythms Improve Motor Synchronization?

The Question: Rhythmic auditory stimulation (RAS) uses external auditory cues to support motor timing and has been studied in neurologic rehabilitation. I wanted to test whether adding structured rhythmic accents could improve synchronization compared with a standard metronome.

What I Built: I developed a Python-based audio generation system that produces custom accented rhythmic stimuli, then designed a 25-participant pilot using a finger-tapping synchronization task.

What I Observed: In this pilot dataset, the accented-rhythm condition showed an 18.4% lower mean timing error than the standard metronome condition.

Why I'm Continuing: The result suggests that rhythmic structure may influence auditory-motor synchronization. Larger, controlled studies are needed to test whether the effect replicates and whether related approaches could eventually have rehabilitation applications.

[ 07 / Response Control ]

Rhythmic Distractor Test

What It Is: An exploratory response-control task. A main pulse plays every 600 ms while unpredictable higher-pitched distractor tones occur between beats.

Why It's Included: The task examines whether rhythmic distraction changes behavioral response control and synchronization. It does not directly measure neural inhibition.

01 Follow Main Pulse

Tap only when you hear the lower-pitched target beat.

02 Ignore Distractors

Do not tap on the higher-pitched off-beat tones.

  • Experimental Condition: Accented rhythmic stimulus at the same target interval with additional structural accents.

  • Purpose: Feasibility testing and preliminary comparison of synchronization under two auditory conditions.
This page reports a preliminary pilot. The current project does not claim statistical significance or clinical efficacy.
[ 10 / Limitations ]

Limitations & Next Steps

This initial pilot was designed to evaluate feasibility and identify preliminary patterns rather than establish clinical efficacy. The sample was limited to 25 participants, and browser/device latency may introduce variability in millisecond-level timing measurements.

Next steps include a larger sample, tighter device and experimental controls, additional rhythmic conditions, formal statistical testing, and analysis of individual response variability.

NeuroPulse has not yet been evaluated in a clinical population.
